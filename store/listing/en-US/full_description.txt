Hold down and the ship digs. Everything below you is rock until you cut it, and everything you cut is a shaft you have to climb back up.

A mining game about depth, fuel, and knowing when to turn around.

THERE IS SOMETHING DOWN THERE
Barriers cut the planet into three depths, and nothing in the ship can touch them. Three Anchors hold each one, buried in halls you have to find. Break all three and a dark core grows in the barrier, warm and inviting and the only way through. Break that and the way down opens, and something of it comes aboard the ship. The last barrier is the door of the Vault at the center. Three marks under the depth readout fill as you break a depth's Anchors, so the game never stops telling you what it wants.

DIG WHERE YOU LIKE
There is no route. The whole face is diggable, so the shaft is whatever shape you make it, and the one you cut on the way down is the one you climb on the way back. Cutting straight is fast and cutting wide finds more.

FUEL IS THE CLOCK
Nothing hunts you, but the ground is not safe. Gas pockets open the hull, deep rock cooks it, and tremors close tunnels behind you. What actually limits a run is the tank, and every meter costs the same going down as coming up. Turning around with half a tank left is the correct play and it never feels like one.

THE HOLD FILLS UP
Copper near the surface, then iron, silver and gold, and further down amethyst, emerald, ruby, and things with no names you would recognize. Weight is real: a full hold climbs slower and burns more. Deciding what to leave in the rock is the decision the whole game is built on.

ROCK THAT IS HOLDING SOMETHING UP
Below ninety meters you will start seeing a warm glow in the stone that is worth more than anything else down there. Cutting it pays better than any ore at that depth, and it brings the tunnel you came down in partly down behind you, so the way home is one you have to find again. Leave it and you walked past the richest thing on the descent, and you could see it the whole time. Both answers cost you something, which is the point of it.

SOMEBODY ELSE GOT HERE FIRST
Twelve wrecked drill ships are buried across the planet, one in every region, always in the same places. You will see the shape of one in the dark before you know what it is: flat plates where the ground has none, and its own lamp still faintly lit at the end that went in first. Cut through and the hold still holds whatever that crew had dug before they stopped. It asks you for nothing. It is just a thing that happened down here before you arrived.

THE ROCK GIVES THINGS UP
Eight devices are buried out there and none of them is for sale. A cutting laser, a salvage magnet, a repair drone, and a receiver that hears an unlit Anchor through solid rock and tells you how near it is, never which way. You still have to choose a direction and dig it.

SPEND IT BETWEEN RUNS
Sell the haul at the landing pad and put it into the ship. A bigger tank, a better drill, a stronger hull, a lamp that reaches further. Each one buys depth, and depth is what the Anchors are waiting in. Every barrier you open has a shop and a save point of its own.

THE PLANET NOTICES
Break the first core and the ground stops staying where you left it, and the planet starts to come apart. Every Anchor after that makes it come apart faster. You can patch it by packing ore into the scars the Anchors leave behind. The back half of the game is dug through a world that is moving.
